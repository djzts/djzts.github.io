abstract: "This paper studies coordinated Large Language Model (LLM) training-task scheduling and facility operation for geographically distributed cloud data centers (CDCs). The central question is how two scheduling flexibilities - cross-CDC task transfer and task reordering - affect service and economic outcomes when remote execution remains subject to compute, bandwidth, energy, and zone-temperature constraints. We formulate a mixed-integer optimization model that jointly determines task outcomes, heterogeneous-node assignment, execution timing, pre-execution data transfer, node activation, and facility operation. A deterministic 24-hour case study contains 3 CDCs, 15 heterogeneous graphics processing unit (GPU) nodes, 4 thermal zones per CDC, and 103 tasks. In this instance, enabling transfer increases net profit by 57.9% relative to the otherwise matched no-transfer variant and increases completed tasks from 76 to 103. The more restrictive no-transfer FIFO-prefix variant completes 14 tasks and yields negative profit. The proposed schedule transfers 39 tasks, achieves 70.3% node utilization, and maintains zone temperatures within 24{\\deg}C-27{\\deg}C. These fixed-instance results indicate that cross-CDC transfer and task-reordering flexibility can improve the modeled profit and task-completion outcomes under the studied conditions."
